# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Three audiences share the front door, and the site has to serve all three without making any of them wade through content meant for another:

- **Single-parent families in Central Florida seeking help.** They may land here directly, in the middle of a real crisis (a utility bill, an eviction notice, empty cupboards), needing to know quickly that this is real and how to reach someone. This is a co-primary audience, not a passive beneficiary group the site talks about in the abstract.
- **Prospective members and donors.** People discovering KOTHOM for the first time who arrive undecided and need to be convinced the ministry is real and worth trusting before they give.
- **The existing congregation and community.** People already connected to the ministry, returning to check in, give again, or find information.

## Product Purpose

Knights of the Higher Order Ministries is a Christian non-profit, founded and led by Pastor Andrew S. Trexler ("Pastor T"), dedicated to assisting single-parent families and their children in Central Florida with the struggles of life. The ministry works through direct contact with single-parent families, learning of their struggles and formulating a mutually agreed-upon plan — offering direct financial assistance with utility bills, clothing costs, food, and housing, alongside pastoral services available by call or appointment 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, 365 days a year. The site serves two directions at once: it is where a family in need finds a fast, clear way to ask for help, and where a supporter finds a fast, clear way to give. Giving happens through two distinct paths: annual membership ("Become a Knight," $25+/year, includes a Knights of the Higher Order T-shirt) and planned/estate giving ("Legacy Donations," for wills, living trusts, and beneficiary designations). Success is either a completed gift through either giving path, or a family in need successfully making contact.

## Positioning

"Spreading His Word, One Family At A Time" — a ministry built on salvation, with a desire to help single-parent families and their children. Every page should reinforce that a gift here (of any size, immediate or planned) directly funds concrete help — a utility bill paid, groceries bought, a family kept housed — for a real family in Central Florida, and that a family in need can reach a real person quickly.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA (donors): Become a Knight — an annual membership donation of $25 or more, which includes a Knights of the Higher Order T-shirt. This is one of two routes into membership.
- Primary CTA (families in need): Get Help — a distinct, clearly-labeled path to request assistance, separate from donation language, so someone in crisis doesn't have to parse "donate" framing to find it.
- Second route to membership (not a fallback, a parallel ask for a different donor segment): Legacy Donations — planned/estate giving via will, living trust, or beneficiary designation. Donors who give this way also become Knights, the same as an annual gift; this path exists for donors thinking about long-term impact rather than (or in addition to) an annual gift.
- Tertiary CTA: Pastoral Services — contact for pastoral care by call or appointment, available 24/7, for visitors whose need is spiritual support rather than financial assistance or giving.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: this is a real, working ministry that puts your gift directly into the hands of single-parent families struggling with bills, food, and housing — and if that's you, help is a phone call away.
- Belief ladder (donor side): this ministry is real and legitimate (a named pastor, a physical address, a working phone and email, an existing domain) → its mission is concrete, not abstract (direct financial help with specific, named struggles) → my gift funds that specific help, whether given now or planned into an estate → either way, giving makes me a Knight — visibly (the membership T-shirt for an annual gift) and lastingly (a legacy gift is a standing act of membership too, not a separate track).
- Belief ladder (families-in-need side): this ministry is real and reachable → they help with the specific problem I have (bills, food, housing) → reaching out is safe and fast (a phone number and a name, not a form into a void).
- Proof on hand: no testimonials or press yet, but strong legitimacy signals already exist and should be surfaced prominently: founder/pastor name (Andrew S. Trexler, "Pastor T"), physical address (380 Lake Ontario Court, Altamonte Springs, FL 32701), phone (689-327-6388), email, and an existing registered domain (knightsofthehigherorderministries.org). These function as proof of legitimacy in place of testimonials until real stories are collected.

## Brand Personality

Bold, noble, resolute. The existing Canva-designed materials (glowing radiant cross logo, inscription-style capital lettering, "Knights"/"Higher Order" language) already carry this identity — the site should preserve and extend it, not soften it into generic warm-nonprofit visual language.

## Anti-references

Generic SaaS/startup landing page conventions (gradient text, glassmorphism, hero-metric templates, tiny uppercase eyebrows). Also avoid the flashy, high-pressure prosperity-gospel megachurch look (loud stock photography, autoplay video, aggressive donate popups) — this ministry's existing materials are dignified and text-forward, not spectacle-driven.

## Design Principles

- Serve both directions of the front door equally: a family in crisis finding help and a donor finding how to give are both primary journeys, not one primary and one secondary.
- Preserve the existing identity: the black-and-cream duotone, the radiant cross mark, and the inscription-style capital lettering already established in the ministry's own Canva materials are the brand, not a placeholder to redesign away.
- Name the giving and help paths distinctly: annual membership (Become a Knight), planned giving (Legacy Donations), and requesting help (Get Help) serve different people and should never be collapsed into one generic button.
- Legitimacy is proof: in the absence of testimonials, the founder's name, address, phone, and years of operation carry the trust-building weight — surface them, don't bury them in a footer.
- Bold and noble, not saccharine: lean into the chivalric "Higher Order" identity rather than generic warm-nonprofit visual clichés.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA: sufficient color contrast, full keyboard navigation, and reduced-motion support for any animation. Particular care on contrast given the older, less tech-savvy target audience — body text and buttons should exceed the AA minimum where practical, not just clear it. A family in crisis reading this site may also be under stress; the "Get Help" path should be findable without careful reading.
